# Visual inspiration

## `tabletop-diorama-01.webp`: target look for the **Tabletop** style (and the life layer)
A hand-painted 3D Carcassonne board. What to take from it:
- **Tiles are thick slabs** (about 8–10% of tile width) with cream/beige cut sides. The board reads as physical pieces sitting on a white table, with visible seams and a slight per-tile height variance.
- **Fields:** a saturated, painterly green with a fine grass/flock texture. Small dark-green bush clusters are scattered along roads and field edges.
- **Roads:** pale cream/sand ribbons, slightly sunken and soft-edged, curving organically (not ruler-straight), meeting at small junctions.
- **Cities:**
  - stone curtain walls follow the exact city boundary, with crenellations, round and square towers at corners and wall joints, gatehouses where roads enter, and stairs up the inside of walls,
  - packed tan/ochre courtyard ground inside the walls,
  - **dense clusters of small houses with terracotta-red roofs** filling the city area, irregular in size, rotation and height,
  - small multi-tile cities feel like one walled town; city caps are a wall arc with a few houses.
- **Buildings outside cities:** cloisters and farms are larger cream buildings with red roofs and arcades, sitting on their own plinth.
- **Meeples:** chunky, matte, solid-colour wooden figures, scaled large relative to the houses (readability beats realism). Farmers lie down per the 3rd edition.
- **Lighting:** soft, warm, diffuse daylight with gentle ambient occlusion in the wall/house clusters. A shallow depth of field at the far edge (tilt-shift) sells the miniature feel.
